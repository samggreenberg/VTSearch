Street signs for pictures

How should VTSearch turn an image into the little text Toponymy needs — across photos, screenshots, and scanned documents, and especially when the user is browsing the results of a Find?

The audio study answered whether Toponymy street signs work and shipped a recipe: zero-shot tags against one big generic vocabulary. This study asked whether that recipe survives contact with images — and it mostly doesn't. Image corpora are far more heterogeneous than audio (photos, app screens, faxes), and the browsing entry point is often a fine-grained Find result where a fixed vocabulary is either breed-blind or full of distractors. The short version: fixed tag vocabularies swing between excellent and garbage depending on whether the content happens to be in-vocabulary; an instructed ~3 B VLM captioner is the only strategy that was robust everywhere we pointed it.

Verdicts.
  • Ship object_to_text = instructed VLM caption (Qwen2.5-VL-3B class) as the image default; it was top-2 on every dataset and the only variant that never produced junk signs. Zero-shot SigLIP tags stay as the no-extra-model fallback — good for photos, breed-blind and distractor-prone off-domain.
  • Skip pregenerated naming lists. Directly confirmed: vocabulary tags collapse exactly when signposts matter most — on fine-grained Find results (0–14% breed-sign hit for OpenImages-600 on dogs) and off-domain corpora (94% of its tag vocabulary on screenshots/documents is photo-vocabulary distractors).
  • Subset (Find→Browse) signposting is interactively cheap: re-fitting Toponymy on a 150-image subset takes ~16 s + ~12 LLM calls — signs can be built per-Find, from the subset's own texts, with no dependence on any dataset-level label list.
  • For screenshots and documents the bottleneck is the map, not the names: SigLIP clusters UI screens by look, not function (ARI 0.07 vs Enrico's 20 design topics), and document types only weakly (0.39). VLM-caption signs still honestly describe what is in each region. The audio study's text-space rescue does not transfer at caption granularity — a useful negative result. See Finding 6.

Metrics per layer: cluster quality vs ground truth (ARI/NMI/purity — identical across text variants, since clustering only sees the SigLIP matrix), and name quality: a topic's sign "hits" when its text is closer to the cluster's majority label than 95% of random name–label pairs. We report the neutral-encoder hit rate (MiniLM) because SigLIP-space similarity structurally favors names composed of SigLIP vocabulary terms; the SigLIP-space numbers are in the eval JSONs and are uniformly kinder to the tag variants.

FINDING 1For ordinary photos, the audio recipe ports as-is

On Caltech-101 the SigLIP map is essentially the category structure (fine layer: ARI 0.92, purity 0.93 over 96 topics), and every text strategy names it well — OpenImages tags reach 77% neutral-encoder hit rate, ImageNet-21k 91%, Florence/Qwen captions 92%, against an oracle bound of 93%. Sign quality differences barely matter when the corpus is generic photos of distinct objects: this is the ESC-50 of images, and it works out of the box with zero extra models.

FINDING 2On a mixed corpus, coarse signs are basically solved

This is the "browsing the entire dataset" half of the use case, and it is not where the strategies differentiate. Every variant's coarse layer separated the four domains essentially perfectly, because the clustering never sees the text — and at 4–6 clusters even mediocre per-image text mines enough contrastive signal for a domain-level name. (OpenImages tags label the document island "fax envelope filing cabinet" — inelegant, but a user would still know what's there.)

FINDING 3Fixed vocabularies fail off-domain — and the failure is quantifiable before any clustering

The distractor half of the problem is just as measurable. Classifying each strategy's emitted terms by domain (SigLIP text-space, photo/document/screenshot probes): on RVL-CDIP documents, 93% of the OpenImages tag vocabulary in use is photo-object terms — "pen", "envelope", "lily", "taxi" — and on Enrico screenshots 94%. The huge vocabulary is not a fix: ImageNet-21k on screenshots surfaces WordNet exotica ("guesser", "sodalist", "hodoscope") instead. Your intuition about the dog-breed labels polluting the faxed-spreadsheet detector is exactly what happens, in both directions:

FINDING 4The Find→Browse subset is where strategies live or die

Two operational notes from these runs. First, the subset fits are fast: ~16 s end-to-end for 150 images plus ~12 naming calls (≈6 s with a resident 7B; milliseconds with the keyphrase namer) — building signs per-Find is interactive, and the per-image texts can be cached at ingest like thumbnails, so only the clustering + naming re-runs. Second, the map itself gets better at subset grain (10-breed subset: ARI 0.88 / purity 0.90 vs 0.44 / 0.50 on all 120 breeds) — zoomed-in SigLIP structure is real, so good subset signs are there to be had if the text carries the distinctions.

Quantitatively, on dogs the LLM namer lowered the in21k hit rate from 76% → 50%: when the keyphrases are already exact terms (breed names), the 7B model's paraphrase hedges toward genericity. And names derived from captions inherit the caption's axis: captions describe appearance, so some signs drift from breed to looks ("Fluffy White Dogs"). Meanwhile duplicate-sign rates were ~0–3% across the board (vs 38% on hard audio maps) — image maps don't need the LLM for dedup nearly as much. The namer is worth having for phrasing and for messy text (OCR, long captions); it should be instructed to preserve exact rare terms (breeds, brands, form types) rather than generalize them.

FINDING 6Screenshots and documents: the names are fine — the map and the metric are the problem

Every variant scores terribly on Enrico (≤23%) and RVL-CDIP (≤11%) against their official taxonomies. Two distinct things are going on:

(a) The SigLIP map doesn't encode those taxonomies. Enrico's fine layer has ARI 0.07 against the 20 design topics — the map organizes screens by visual style, not function, exactly as the audio study found CLAP organizing speech by acoustics, not words (ARI 0.05). No naming strategy can fix cluster membership. (b) The hit metric only credits taxonomy words. The signs the VLM+LLM produce for document clusters are things like Material Safety Data Sheets for Industrial Chemicals, Tobacco Institute Financial Transactions, Handwritten letters expressing consumer dissatisfaction — honest, specific, and arguably better navigation aids than "letter" or "memo", but scored as misses.

The audio study's resolution for speech was "browse the transcript embeddings instead," and we tested the image analog: embed each image's caption/OCR text with E5 and hand Toponymy that as both the map and the clusterable space. It does not transfer. On RVL-CDIP, text-space clustering left document-type structure flat (ARI 0.39 → 0.38; OCR-only text was worse, 0.29); on Enrico it nudged ARI 0.07 → 0.09 and roughly doubled the sign–label hit rate (13% → 32%) — better naming alignment on an unchanged-quality map. The audio analogy fails because a transcript is the semantic content of speech, while a one-line caption compresses away exactly the layout/type detail that separates an invoice from a memo; E5 then clusters the captions by topical words (brands, chemicals) rather than by document type. The honest conclusion: for documents, taxonomy-grade structure likely needs a layout- or document-aware embedder (Donut/LayoutLM-class), which is follow-up work — while for browsing, the content-axis clusters the current pipeline produces, with VLM signs on them, are already navigable.

Captions are a per-image, embarrassingly-cacheable cost — computed once at ingest (like thumbnails) they make every later browse, subset browse, and re-name free of VLM calls. The 3B VLM fits alongside SigLIP in ~8 GB; on CPU-only deployments the fallback chain is tags (photos) / OCR-caption via the existing converters (docs).

What this says about the roadmap

  • Phase 3 object_to_text for images should be a captioner, not a vocabulary. Default: instructed ~3B VLM one-liner (type + subject + key visible text), cached at ingest. Fallbacks: SigLIP tags vs OpenImages-600 (no new models; fine for photo corpora), and the existing document/image OCR converters for text-heavy media. Do not ship a curated per-domain label list as the primary path — Findings 3–4 are direct evidence against it.
  • Wire subset signposting into Find→Browse from day one. It's ~16 s for a 150-image result set reusing cached texts; signs computed on the subset are strictly better than filtered dataset-level signs (the contrastive keyphrases recompute against the subset's own siblings). In-memory only, per the existing plan (G3).
  • LLM namer prompt needs a "preserve exact terms" instruction (breeds, brands, form titles) — plus the audio study's length/English constraints. Keep KeyphraseNamer as the no-LLM default; image maps showed ~0% duplicate signs, so its main audio weakness doesn't carry over.
  • Don't build caption-text-space browsing expecting the audio rescue — measured: flat on document types, marginal on screens (Finding 6). If taxonomy-grade document structure becomes a requirement, the path is a document-aware embedder (Donut/LayoutLM-class) as a selectable image embedder, not a re-plumbing of the caption text.
  • Fix the image demo datasets — Stanford Dogs loads 0 images and re-downloads 750 MB per load (#2290), the RVL-CDIP mirror is single-class (#2291), Enrico is doubly rotted (#2292). Fixes are prototyped in the experiment framework.
  • Open follow-ups: validate the captioner default on an uncurated real-world image dump (the Clotho analog); a 20k-scale run to confirm the base_min_cluster_size ∝ n rule for images; try a 7–8B VLM captioner for breed-grade specificity vs the 3B (the "Fluffy White Dogs" clusters); measure caption cache size vs pickle bloat.

Method & caveats

Caveats: (1) sign–label hit rate measures agreement with each dataset's official taxonomy; Finding 6 shows this understates real utility on documents/screens. (2) Demo datasets are curated and single-domain except mixed, which is synthetic; an uncurated dump remains untested. (3) The Qwen captioner was tested with one prompt; prompt sensitivity unmeasured. (4) Florence-2 ran with use_cache=False under transformers 4.57 (2× slower than native). (5) Per-breed n=15 in the full Dogs run is small; the subset run (n=20/breed) is the cleaner read.
