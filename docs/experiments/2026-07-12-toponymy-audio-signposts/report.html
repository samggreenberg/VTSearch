VTSearch · experiment report · 2026-07-12

Street signs for sound

Can Toponymy name the neighborhoods of an audio browse map well enough that a user finds what they want without listening to every clip? And what is the right way to turn a 5-second sound into the "little text" the naming pipeline needs?

ESC-50 + Speech Commands + Clotho (4,445 clips) · HLTCOE grid, one a100 · toponymy 0.5.2, LAION-CLAP, Whisper, Qwen2.5-7B · framework: scripts/experiments/toponymy_audio/
4,445audio clips, 3 datasets
4audio→text strategies
2namers: no-LLM / local 7B
≈5 mintags→signs, 2k clips, 1 GPU

The VTSBrowse Toponymy plan (docs/plans/vtsbrowse-toponymy.md) adopts the Tutte Institute toponymy library for hierarchical, contrastive region names — "street signs" on the UMAP canvas — and leaves exactly two things unresolved: the dependency decision and the object_to_text gap (audio has no words; Toponymy needs a little text per object to mine keyphrases from). This report runs the full proposed architecture end-to-end on real audio with ground truth and answers both, plus the question behind the feature: are the signs good enough to navigate by?

Bottom line: the plan's architecture works, and the signposts are genuinely navigable on environmental audio. On ESC-50 (2,000 clips, 50 hidden categories), the finest sign layer matches the human taxonomy at ARI 0.87 / purity 0.97, and 91% of signs semantically match their region's true category — roughly 4 out of 5 clips sit under a correct street sign, with zero audio listened to and zero labels used.

Answer to "how do we turn audio into text": CLAP zero-shot tags against the 527-term AudioSet vocabulary as the default (free, no new models); Whisper transcripts for speech datasets; and — the surprise of the night — a 1 GB audio captioner that captions 2,000 clips in 90 s and gives the most natural signs on uncurated audio. A matched-but-tiny vocabulary is worse than a big generic one.

One hard blocker: pip install toponymy into the app venv would downgrade transformers 5.12→4.57 (the library pins transformers<5). Integration needs a vendored core, an upstream fix, or a sidecar — see roadmap.

EXPERIMENT ADoes a CLAP browse map have nameable neighborhoods at all?

Environmental audio: yes, emphatically. The fine layer's clusters essentially are the ESC-50 taxonomy (purity 0.97) — CLAP does the heavy lifting, and Toponymy's cluster tree recovers a sensible "50 categories → mid-groups → coarse regions" hierarchy without being told 50 exists. Speech: no. ARI 0.05 means the map itself is close to random with respect to what was said — CLAP hears "male speech, television" everywhere, and no namer can fix a map with no structure (37% of clips land in no cluster at all). VTSearch's other audio embedder (ParaSpeechCLAP) targets speaking style, not content, so it doesn't rescue this either. For speech corpora the right browse map is over transcript embeddings — VTSearch already has the audio2text converter path for exactly this.

Four findings, one per strategy:

1 — CLAP AudioSet tags are the right default. Free (the embedder's own text branch + one 527-term embed), and 84–94% of signs land on the true category. Their one failure mode is junk-tag leakage: AudioSet includes music-genre terms CLAP misfires on, and the LLM faithfully works them into signs — Fireworks and Explosions with Flamenco, Buzzing Insects in Bluegrass, Engine Knocking and Tapping Guitar Techniques. Two cheap fixes both work: dropping to top-3 tags removed nearly all of it in the ablation (Flamenco and Bluegrass vanish from every layer's signs; one stray "Guitar" survives) and lifted the LLM's coarse-layer hit rate from 0.38 to 0.62 — and filtering music-genre terms out of the vocabulary for non-music datasets would finish the job.

2 — A matched-but-tiny vocabulary is worse than a big generic one. The "oracle" run tags with the dataset's own 50 category names — surely ideal? No: hit rate drops from 0.84 to 0.66 (LLM namer). With 5 sounds per label-worth of vocabulary, a clip's runner-up tags are semantically distant junk ("crying_baby → cow, hen, sheep"), and the contrastive keyphrase machinery amplifies exactly what distinguishes clusters — including the junk: Thunderstorms with Cows and Frogs, Engine Flush Frog Pig. AudioSet's redundant synonym clusters (Bark/Yip/Dog…) act as error-correction. Vocabulary richness, not domain match, is what matters — ship the big vocab.

3 — Whisper is mandatory for speech and useless elsewhere. On Speech Commands it transcribes the spoken word (3% empty; the no-LLM namer then names clusters literally marvin, dog, right — the best possible sign). On ESC-50's non-speech audio it is a disaster in an instructive way: 38% of clips transcribe to nothing, and most of the rest is hallucination — Sinhala/Georgian character runs on airplane noise, and Whisper's training-data watermarks ("ご視聴ありがとうございました", "구독과 좋아요는…" — YouTube outro phrases) surfacing as transcripts. The signs it yields are the worst of the whole study (hitST 0.14, 46% duplicates; one accidental gem: woof woof woof woof). It also cost 34 minutes for 2,000 clips — 20× the captioner — because non-speech triggers Whisper's temperature-fallback retries. Media-type routing, not a universal default.

4 — The audio captioner is the sleeper hit. MU-NLPC/whisper-small-audio-captioning is a 1 GB checkpoint that captioned all 2,000 ESC-50 clips in 90 s (and 1,045 longer Clotho clips in ~60 s) on the a100 — the plan had written captioning off as "optional, heavy," but it is neither. Captions read like human descriptions ("A dog is chewing on something and snoring.") and produce the most natural signs on uncurated audio (Experiment D). By the neutral metric it is the best all-rounder on ESC-50 — hitST 0.84 / 0.91 / 0.69 across fine/mid/coarse with the LLM namer, the only strategy that holds up at the coarse layer — and its signs need no junk filtering at all: Toilet Flush and Drain, Cat Meowing Variations, Saw Cutting Wood Sounds, Baby Crying Variations.

EXPERIMENT CNamers: is the no-LLM fallback honest, and what does a local 7B buy?

The plan's Phase 1 ships a KeyphraseNamer — a trivial LLMWrapper that answers every naming prompt with the cluster's top contrastive keyphrase, no model at all. Phase 2 swaps in a real LLM. Both were run over the same fits (LLM: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, bf16, in-process via toponymy's own HuggingFaceNamer):

The no-LLM fallback is more than honest — on tag-based texts it often scores better than the LLM (its names are made of the same vocabulary the metric's category names live in; the independent-encoder columns above shrink but don't erase the gap). Its real costs are cosmetic: raw lowercase keyphrase strings, and no dedup power — on Speech Commands 38% of its layer-0 signs are duplicates (marvin × 3), versus 0% for the LLM. The LLM buys: fluent title-case signs, abstraction over tag lists, dedup — and three new failure modes worth engineering around: (a) it faithfully launders junk tags into fluent nonsense ("with Flamenco"), (b) coarse layers sometimes collapse to fillers like Sounds (the null name is a known LLM-namer trap), (c) Qwen occasionally answers in Chinese (日语学习指令 on a speech cluster) — llm_specific_instructions must pin output language.

Cost: naming is one LLM call per topic. ESC-50's 117 topics took 113 calls ≈ 55 s of GPU (≈2 topics/s, unbatched); the whole tags→signs pipeline lands around 3–5 minutes for 2,000 clips including two UMAP fits. Note the scaling trap: topic count ≈ n / base_min_cluster_size, so 50k clips at the default min-cluster-size of 10 would mean ~5,000 LLM calls (~40 GPU-min). The min cluster size must scale with n (or use toponymy's async/batch namers).

Default fine-layer prompts ask for "domain expert level (8 to 15 word)" names — good topic-model labels, bad street signs. For the map, raise lowest_detail_level (names shrink toward "clear and concise, 3–6 words") and give the LLM a sign-length instruction. Also worth knowing: Toponymy names layers strictly from texts/keyphrases — it never asks the LLM to look at the parent name — so the "child refines parent" reading on the canvas emerges from the shared cluster tree, which held up well in practice here.

EXPERIMENT DThe map test: browsing without listening

The point of all of this is the canvas. Below is the actual 2-D UMAP layout (points colored by hidden ground truth, invisible to the pipeline) with Toponymy's signs placed at each topic's 2-D medoid — a faithful mock of what VTSBrowse would render at mid-zoom:

Reading the ESC-50 map: a user hunting for "typing sounds" pans past Keyboard & Sewing Sounds and is done — under that sign, 97% of clips really are keyboard/mouse-click recordings. Composite "navigability" — the chance a random clip is inside a cluster whose sign correctly describes it (coverage × purity × name-hit) — is ≈0.82 at the fine layer, ≈0.64 mid-zoom on ESC-50 with the free tags+no-LLM configuration. The equivalent number on the speech map is ≈0.04: unnavigable, as Experiment A predicted.

Clotho is the realistic case — 1,045 uncurated Freesound recordings, no categories, exactly the "pile of unlabeled clips" VTSearch users bring. With captioner texts + LLM namer the four coarse signs are Environment Interactions, Urban and Rural Bird Sounds, Machine and Vehicle Sounds, Water Sounds; mid-zoom splits into 11 sensible districts (Crowded Place Chatter, Train Passing Sounds, Person Walking on Various Surfaces…). Spot-checking members against Clotho's human captions confirms the signs describe their regions. Noise is honest here too: 26–35% of clips sit between islands — real-world audio genuinely is that diverse, and the map says so instead of forcing membership.

What this says about the roadmap

Adopt the library, but not via pip install into the app venv (yet). toponymy 0.5.2 hard-pins transformers<5.0.0 (plus unconditional heavyweight deps: datasets, dask, vectorizers, apricot-select, fast_hdbscan); a uv dry-run against the current venv shows it downgrading transformers 5.12.1→4.57.6 and huggingface-hub 1.20.1→0.36.2 — not acceptable for the app. In order of preference: (1) upstream fix — the transformers import is only really exercised by HuggingFaceNamer; propose making it lazy/optional and testing against transformers 5 (PR-sized); (2) vendor the core — clustering + keyphrases + templates are ~3k lines with no transformers dependency, and the namer wrappers we need are exactly the thin LLMWrapper shims this experiment already wrote; (3) sidecar venv/process. Re-check the pin before building anything — the library is actively developed.

Follow-ups worth an experiment before the frontend work (the framework reruns any of these in minutes): vocab filtering ablation (drop music terms → does "Flamenco" vanish?); captioner on a second uncurated corpus + music (GTZAN); larger-scale run (urbansound8k_a or a 20k mix) to validate the mcs scaling rule and naming-cost model; a dedup pass for KeyphraseNamer; detail-level sweep for sign length. Everything else in the plan (G1–G5 glue, persistence, API, canvas rendering) is unblocked by these results.

Method & caveats

Setup. All experiments ran on one HLTCOE grid a100 node (rack8n05) against the vtscore library tier — the same demo downloader, CLAP embedder (laion/clap-htsat-unfused, 512-d, deterministic first-10 s window), and text branch the app uses; no Flask. Toponymy 0.5.2 with its example defaults (ToponymyClusterer(min_clusters=4), information_weighted keyphrases, central exemplars, cosine 5-D UMAP, seed 42). Texts are precomputed and passed as the objects list — for per-clip strings this is equivalent to the object_to_text hook and lets keyphrases mine all clips, not just exemplars. Namer LLM: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct bf16 in-process; toponymy's own retry/parse stack handled every response (147 keyphrase / 113 LLM calls on ESC-50, zero hard failures).

Metrics. ARI/NMI/purity over non-noise clips vs. hidden categories; name–label agreement = cosine(sign text, majority category name), embedded with CLAP text (biased toward tag-made names) and MiniLM (neutral); "hit" = sim above the 95th percentile of that sign's similarity to 20 random categories; dup = 1 − unique(signs)/signs.

Caveats. Single seed (42) per fit — UMAP/HDBSCAN variance untested; ESC-50 is curated and clean, so its headline numbers are an upper bound (Clotho is the honest preview); Qwen sampled at toponymy's default temperature, so names vary run to run; the CLAP-space name metric favors tag-derived names (hence the second encoder); the captioner was trained on Clotho-style data, flattering Experiment D's Clotho result (its strong ESC-50 captions are the counter-evidence); 3–10 s clips only — long-form audio needs the clipper first. Grid ops notes (exclusive-process GPUs, node-local scratch, HF cache traps) are in the framework README.

Reproduce / extend. scripts/experiments/toponymy_audio/ — park a GPU allocation, setup_node.sh, then prepare_dataset.py → make_texts.py → run_toponymy.py → evaluate.py → visualize.py per dataset/variant/namer; each stage is a JSON file on disk, so new strategies slot in as new texts_*.json writers.
