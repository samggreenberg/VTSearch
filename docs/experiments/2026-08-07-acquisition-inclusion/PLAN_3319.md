## What #3318 left open

PR #3318 shipped `ACQUISITION_INCLUSION_OFFSET = -3` on `vg_scale_any × siglip ×
whole_image`, 192 paired cells, against the then-shipped `-1`:

| arm | Δ final cost vs `-1` | Δ positives@100 | Δ AP | deep spikes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `-2` | −0.013 [−0.026, −0.012] | +4.5 | +0.020 | 0.0% → 1.0% |
| `-3` | **−0.020** [−0.029, −0.011] | +10 | +0.045 | 0.0% → 0.0% |
| `-4` | −0.017 [−0.030, −0.013] | **+18** | **+0.057** | 0.0% → 0.5% |

`-4` is at least as good as `-3` on every endpoint and the trend has not turned.
So **`-4` is the edge of the grid, not an optimum**, and `-3` shipped as the
conservative choice because `-4` costs a small resolvable regression under region
voting (+0.006, CI [+0.001, +0.013]) where `-3` is free.

Three questions follow. None is answerable from those cells.

## What a value MEANS — the frame this study reads its result in

Worth stating before the arms, because it is what makes a *fractional* grid
principled rather than a fishing expedition, and because the whole study is an
argument about where a point on this scale should sit.

`inclusion_cost_weights` defines the knob as a loss over the two error **rates**:
`cost = w_fp·FPR + w_fn·FNR`, with `(w_fp, w_fn) = (2^-k, 1)` for `k ≤ 0`.
Because each error is normalised by its own class, **the prevalence divides
out** — deliberately: `GmmFit1D.rate_crossing` puts the prior-odds factor back
into `lam` precisely so the realised cut does not carry it, which is what #2836
shipped. Differentiating the loss leaves the stationarity condition
`w_fn·f_pos(x) = w_fp·f_neg(x)`, i.e.

> **include *x* ⟺ f_pos(x) / f_neg(x) > 2^−k**

So inclusion is a **log₂ likelihood-ratio threshold**. `k = 0` is the
neutral-evidence point — admit whatever the Good class explains better than the
Bad class. **Each step of the knob is one bit of evidence** (Good's *weight of
evidence*, in base 2): `-3` asks for 8:1 evidence, `-3.5` for 11.3:1, `+2`
accepts 4:1 against.

Three consequences the study leans on:

1. **It is not "purely mechanical."** The offset has an exact decision-theoretic
   reading, and a portable one — a constant shift in *evidence bits* is
   prior-free and dataset-free. That is the real argument for parameterising this
   as an **offset** rather than an absolute inclusion, and it predicts (correctly,
   so far) that the value should transfer across environments.
2. **The integer grid is an assumption, and a coarse one.** One step *doubles*
   the evidence demanded. Between `-3` and `-4` the cut jumps 8:1 → 16:1 with
   nothing in between, and #3318's own CIs for `-3` and `-4` overlap. If the
   optimum is at `-3.5` the integer grid **cannot see it**, and the
   "conservative `-3` vs aggressive `-4`" framing is an artefact of the spacing.
3. **Prior-free in evidence, prevalence-bound in RANK.** Acquisition does not
   consume the threshold as a decision boundary — it consumes it as a **rank
   position**. The map from bits to rank runs through the fitted mixture, and
   *that* depends on prevalence and on separation. So the same offset lands at
   different rank positions in different environments, and in the *same*
   environment at different points of one trajectory. This is the mechanism
   behind the adaptive **ramp** that #2876 found and `rank_pin` (constant by
   construction) lacks, and it is why `rank_pin` has now failed in five
   environments.

### The landmark this frame predicts (H4)

Put the prior back in for a moment — not into the cut, but into the question
"what is the selector actually picking?". An item at likelihood ratio λ has
posterior odds `λ·π/(1−π)`. At `vg_scale_any`'s designed prevalence **π = 7.1%**
the prior odds are 0.0764, so the *reporting* line at `k = 0` (LR > 1) admits
items that are still **13:1 against** being positive. The selector's picks
become **more likely Good than Bad** only at

> `k* = −log₂((1−π)/π) = −log₂(13.08) = **−3.71**`

That is a real landmark on the same axis the study sweeps, and it sits **between
`-3.5` and `-4`** — inside the half-step grid and invisible to the integer one.
It is a *hypothesis*, not a derivation: acquisition is a sampling policy, not a
decision rule, so nothing forces the optimum to sit at the point where picks flip
from mostly-wrong to mostly-right. But it is the first principled reason anyone
has offered for *why this constant has the value it has*, and it is falsifiable
here.

It also predicts the deep-regime answer, and predicts it **against** the issue's
stated worry — see H3.

## Hypotheses, pre-registered

**H1 — the frontier turns.** On the shipped arm, `final_cost` is non-monotone in
`k` with an interior minimum at or above `-6`. *Falsified* if `-5`, `-6` and `-8`
each improve on `-4`, which would mean the object is not an offset at all but a
separate acquisition rule (the issue's outcome 2, and a shape finding rather
than a parameter one).

**H2 — the optimum is resolvable at finer than one step.** At least one half step
differs from **both** its integer neighbours on the decision endpoint by more
than the ±0.010 tolerance. *Falsified* if every half step sits inside its
neighbours' CIs, which would say the knob's usable resolution is one bit and the
integer grid was right all along — a genuinely useful negative, and the one that
retires the question.

*Prerequisite, already discharged.* A half step must be a distinct operating
point and not a duplicate produced by `snap_cut_to_sample` rounding it onto its
neighbour. Verified before this grid was written, on the shipped
`FoldAnchoredCut` path: the half steps are strictly ordered between their
integer neighbours with no collapse
(`test_fractional_cuts_land_strictly_between_their_integer_neighbours`). The
run-time control is the `acq_pool_percentile` instrument — **an arm whose pool
percentile is identical to a neighbour's in more than 10% of cells is reported as
a duplicate, and its comparison is refused**, exactly as #2876's analyzer refuses
an arm whose percentile never moved.

**H3 — the deep regime deepens the optimum rather than flattening it.** The issue
expects the sign may flip, reasoning that the offset's benefit is concentrated
where positives are scarce (#2910) and deep voting is where scarcity ends. The
likelihood-ratio frame predicts the **opposite**, and the disagreement is worth
naming because it turns on two different scarcities:

* scarcity of **labelled** positives (training signal) — *ends* with deep voting;
* scarcity of **remaining** positives in the unvoted pool — *increases* with deep
  voting, because the aggressive arms have harvested them.

The selector ranks the **unvoted pool**, whose π falls as positives are consumed,
which moves `k*` *more* negative. So H3 predicts the optimal offset at t=400 is
at least as deep as at t=100. *Falsified* if the 400-click optimum is shallower
than the 100-click one.

**H4 — the optimum sits at the posterior-flip landmark**, `k* ≈ −3.7`.
*Supported* if the fitted frontier minimum falls in `[-4.0, -3.5]`; *falsified*
if it falls at `-3` or beyond `-5`. H4 is subordinate to H1: if the frontier does
not turn at all, H4 is moot and is reported as such rather than fitted anyway.

**Falsification arm.** `acq_p2` (`k = +2`) must make positives **worse**. It has
behaved in five environments; without it nothing else here is interpretable, and
an `acq_p2` that does *not* degrade voids the run rather than being explained.

## Endpoints and decision rules

Inherited from #2876/#2877 unchanged, so all five environments stay comparable.

* **Decision endpoint:** `final_cost` at t=100, **paired at the (category, seed)
  cell**, reported as a paired mean delta with a 95% bootstrap CI. Ship tolerance
  **±0.010**.
* **Report both the paired delta and the arm's own median.** They can disagree —
  in #2876 they did for two arms — and the disagreement is the finding, not a
  nuisance. An arm is only *recommended* when both agree.
* **Mechanism:** positives per 100 votes at t=50 and t=100; `final_ap`; oracle
  cost (the best any threshold could do on that ranking, which separates "better
  detector" from "better cut").
* **Guardrails:** deep-spike incidence (`SPIKE_DEEP_COST`, read as a **paired
  contrast** — it is absolute and calibrated to COCO's cost scale, and on
  `vg_scale_any` base rates run 0.5–4.5%, which leaves it live), worst-step
  regret, and threshold stability. #3318's every rejection turned on spikes, not
  cost, and that is expected to remain the binding criterion.
* **Instrument for the lever itself:** `acq_threshold`, `acq_pool_percentile`,
  `report_pool_percentile`. The direction is counter-intuitive, so a sign error
  would look exactly like "the lever does nothing". **An arm whose pool
  percentile did not move is refused, not interpreted.**
* **Frontier fit:** the turning point is read off the paired deltas with their
  CIs, *not* from a smooth fit through them. A parabola through 12 points would
  manufacture an interior optimum whether or not one exists.

**Rows are filtered to base rows** (`gmm_variant == ""`, `pool_variant in ("",
"max")`, no `schedule`) before anything is compared — each step also emits
per-variant rows carrying their own `threshold`.

## Sizing

#3318 measured paired SD **0.065** on `final_cost` on this arm, so n ≈ 164 for a
±0.010 half-width. **16 seeds × 12 categories = 192 cells per arm**, which clears
it with margin and matches #3318 exactly, keeping the two directly comparable.

The deep wave gets **its own pilot** — a 400-step trajectory is not a 100-step
one scaled, and its per-step fit grows with the vote count. Seeds are set from
that measurement, not inherited.

`CALIB_CELL_ORDER=seed` makes the array seed-major, so a top-up is `arms 16-23`
with every existing cell still counting. Under the default `category` ordering,
changing the seed count remaps every index and makes every earlier cell
unresumable — the mistake #2877 could not undo.

## Known hazards, named in advance

* **Positive exhaustion in the deep wave.** At 7.1% prevalence a 2100-media sim
  half holds only ~150 positives; a 400-click trajectory can consume most of
  them and **flatten its own tail**. That artefact reads exactly like "the offset
  stops mattering at depth", which is the finding under test. The wave carries a
  positives-remaining trace and the report states the exhaustion point per arm;
  any deep conclusion drawn past that point is labelled as such or not drawn.
* **The half steps could be duplicates.** Discharged analytically and by test
  above; policed at run time by the `acq_pool_percentile` refusal.
* **Twelve arms is a multiplicity problem.** The decision endpoint is the
  *shipped* comparison (a named incumbent against a named challenger), not a
  scan for the best of twelve. The frontier shape is read descriptively; a ship
  recommendation is made against `-3` alone, at the ±0.010 tolerance.
