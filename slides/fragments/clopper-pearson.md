<!-- **a** — Quick stats detour before the app uses this. Say we pick five
     things at random from a set, and four are right. The obvious guess is 80%.
     But that's five picks. How sure are we? -->

<!-- **b** — Start with the bottom of the range. How low could the true share
     be and still give us four or more right out of five? At 28%, that happens
     one time in forty. Any lower, and what we saw would be a fluke. -->

<!-- **c** — Same thing from the top. If 99.5% were right, getting four or
     fewer out of five happens one time in forty. Any higher, and missing even
     one would be a fluke. -->

<!-- **d** — Everything in between is the range: 28% to 99.5%. That's
     Clopper-Pearson. It's exact, not an approximation, and it's honest about
     tiny samples: five picks buy you a range almost as wide as the whole axis.
     With one in forty off each end, it misses the truth at most one time in
     twenty. -->

<!-- **e** — Same 80%, from fifty picks: 66% to 90%. The share didn't change;
     how sure we are did. Next, the app does exactly this on the kept set. -->
