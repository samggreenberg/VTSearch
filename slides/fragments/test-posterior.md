<!-- One band, the Bayesian way. Home on the Range turned a count into a
     Clopper–Pearson range; Test uses a posterior instead, because posteriors
     from different bands can be drawn together and added up (next slide), and
     a posterior means the same whenever you stop. -->

<!-- **a** — Before any pick: Jeffreys' prior, Beta(½, ½). Every share is
     possible, with a little more weight at the two ends, and the whole prior
     is worth a single pick, so the picks speak almost at once. -->

<!-- **b** — Two right of five: Beta(2½, 3½), the prior plus the rights and
     the wrongs. Its central 95% is 9–79%. Clopper–Pearson on the same picks
     says 5–85%: wider, because it guarantees coverage at every possible
     share. The posterior's coverage is measured instead, on the last slide of
     this section. -->

<!-- **c** — What the app needs is a count, not a share. The band holds 16:
     5 seen, 2 of them right, and the other 11 drawn binomially at a share
     drawn from the posterior. The band likely holds 2–12 matches. A band no
     bigger than a round is simply counted, and its count is exact. -->

<!-- **d** — A second round in the same band, one more right in five: 3 of 10
     seen, likely 3–8. Six unseen instead of eleven, at a tighter share. Two
     slides on, the app swaps Jeffreys' prior for a better one on each side of
     the line. -->
