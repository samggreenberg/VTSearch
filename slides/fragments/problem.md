<!-- Someone has a corpus — a drive of imagery, a video archive, a folder of
     documents — and a concept they can describe to a colleague in one sentence
     and cannot express as a query. Today it is "book". Easy to name, and
     impossible to write down: *find me the books*. -->

<!-- **a** — Start where everybody agrees. A pile, a shelf, one volume face up:
     three different pictures of the same idea, and nobody in the room needs a
     definition to sort them. If this were the whole of it there would be no
     talk. -->

<!-- **b** — Then the arguments, one at a time, because they are not the same
     argument. A magazine rack: printed, bound, shelved, sold in a shop with
     books in it — and most people say no. Ask, and wait for the answer. -->

<!-- **c** — A shelf of DVD box sets. Spines, titles, an author of sorts,
     shelved exactly as books are. This is the one to spend a beat on: COCO's
     annotators labelled those spines *books*, and two slides from now the user
     in this deck votes Bad on this very frame. The data says one thing and the
     person says another, and the person is the one who asked. -->

<!-- **d** — A spiral notebook. Blank, and that is the whole objection: it has
     every physical property of a book and none of the content. Three
     rejections, three unrelated reasons — which is why no rule fixes this. -->

<!-- Not hypothetical: `book` is the dirtiest of the twelve classes we
     re-reviewed for the `vg_scale` dataset — three of twenty sampled negatives
     turned out to hold one, against zero for eight of the others. -->

<!-- Where the concept ends belongs to **the person who asked**, not to the
     data, and no labelled set knows it. The classical answer is to go and
     label a few thousand examples and train something. That costs days, and it
     is useless here — not because it is technically wrong, but because the
     whole task is worth about **twenty minutes** of this person's attention.
     Everything in the talk follows from taking that budget seriously. -->
