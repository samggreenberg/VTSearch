## Judging a Book by its Cover

<!-- Someone has a corpus — a drive of imagery, a video archive, a folder of
     documents — and a concept they can describe to a colleague in one sentence
     and cannot express as a query. Today it is "book". Easy to name, and
     impossible to write down: *find me the books*. -->

<!-- Top row is what anyone means by a book — a pile, a shelf, one volume face
     up. Bottom row is a magazine rack, a boxed set of DVDs and a spiral
     notebook: printed, rectangular, shelved, and every one of them an
     argument. Ask the room which are in. They will not agree — and nothing
     about the pictures settles it, because **where the concept ends belongs to
     the person who asked**, not to the data, and no labelled set knows it. -->

<!-- Not hypothetical: `book` is the dirtiest of the twelve classes we
     re-reviewed for the `vg_scale` dataset — three of twenty sampled negatives
     turned out to hold one, against zero for eight of the others. -->

<!-- The classical answer is to go and label a few thousand examples and train
     something. That costs days, and it is useless here — not because it is
     technically wrong, but because the whole task is worth about **twenty
     minutes** of this person's attention. Everything in the talk follows from
     taking that budget seriously. -->
